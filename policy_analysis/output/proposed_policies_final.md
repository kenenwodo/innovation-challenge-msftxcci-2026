## 1. Elimination of Duration of Status (D/S) for F, J, and I Nonimmigrants

**Affected groups:** F nonimmigrant students, J exchange visitors, I nonimmigrant representatives of foreign information media, F-2 and J-2 family members

**Current policy:** F-1, J-1, and I nonimmigrants are admitted for Duration of Status (D/S), allowing them to remain in the U.S. for the duration of their program without a fixed end date.

**Proposed change:**

DHS proposes to eliminate admitting F-1, J-1, and I nonimmigrant classifications for Duration of Status (D/S). Instead, these nonimmigrants will be admitted for a fixed period tied to their program length or activity, with specific maximum periods. Extensions beyond the admission period require filing an extension of stay (EOS) application with USCIS.

**Plain English:**

Students and exchange visitors will no longer stay indefinitely while their program lasts. Instead, they get a fixed stay period based on their program length and must apply for extensions if needed.

**Allowed:** Admission for period necessary to complete program, up to fixed maximums, Extension of stay by application to USCIS

**Restricted or prohibited:** Admission for Duration of Status (D/S) no longer allowed for F, J, and I nonimmigrants

**Exceptions:** Transition provisions allowing aliens maintaining status before effective date to remain until expiration of EAD or program end date, not exceeding 4 years plus departure period, Extensions permitted via application

**Time limits / durations:** F nonimmigrants can be admitted up to 4 years or length of program on Form I-20, whichever is shorter, 30 days before program start and 30 days after program end (not counted toward max stay), Transition period allowing those admitted for D/S on effective date to remain up to 4 years plus departure periods

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1(a)(4), 8 CFR 214.1(c)(8), 8 CFR 214.1(m), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5), 8 CFR 214.2(j)(1)(ii)

**Source pages:** 1, 36

**Evidence:**

> DHS proposes to amend its regulations to eliminate the practice of admitting F–1 nonimmigrant students, I nonimmigrant representatives of information media, and J–1 exchange visitors (and F–2/J–2 family members) for D/S. The proposed rule would provide for nonimmigrants seeking entry under F, J, or I visas to be admitted for the period required to complete their program, not to exceed the periods of time defined in this proposed rule. The proposed rule would also require nonimmigrants seeking to continue beyond the admission period granted at entry to apply for extension.

> DHS proposes to apply the same fixed period of admission applicable to aliens seeking to change from a different nonimmigrant status to, if eligible, I status.

## 2. Fixed Admission Period for F, J, and I Nonimmigrants

**Affected groups:** F nonimmigrants (academic students), J nonimmigrants (exchange visitors), I nonimmigrants (foreign media representatives)

**Current policy:** F, J, and I nonimmigrants are admitted for Duration of Status (D/S) without a fixed period.

**Proposed change:**

Replace D/S admissions with fixed admission periods tied to program end dates or assignment durations, generally not exceeding 4 years for F and J, and 240 days (90 days for some PRC nationals) for I nonimmigrants, plus arrival and departure grace periods (typically 30 days). Aliens must apply for extension of stay (EOS) with USCIS to remain beyond the fixed period.

**Plain English:**

Students, exchange visitors, and foreign media representatives will receive a fixed time to stay tied to their programs or assignments, plus short grace periods before and after. Staying beyond this requires applying for an extension.

**Allowed:** Admission up to 4 years or program end date, whichever is shorter (F and J), Admission up to 240 days or assignment completion (I nonimmigrants), 90 days for PRC nationals with exceptions, 30-day period before program start for arrival, 30-day period after program end for departure or status maintenance, Applying for extension of stay to remain longer

**Restricted or prohibited:** Admission for Duration of Status (D/S) removed, Unlawful presence accrual after fixed admission period expires, Staying beyond authorized period without approved EOS

**Exceptions:** I nonimmigrants from PRC limited to 90-day admission and extension periods, F and J nonimmigrants admitted for D/S on final rule effective date may continue until program end date plus departure period under transition, Specific provisions for recouping stay after absence up to 30 days in contiguous territory

**Time limits / durations:** Maximum 4-year fixed admission period or program end date for F and J, Admission up to 240 days max for I nonimmigrants; 90 days for some PRC nationals, 30-day arrival and 30-day departure periods, 60 days departure period for F nonimmigrants after admission period; 30 days for J nonimmigrants

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1(a)(4)(i)-(iii), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5), 8 CFR 214.2(j)(1)(ii), 8 CFR 214.1(c)(8), 8 CFR 214.1(m)(1)

**Source pages:** 1, 3, 7, 11, 12, 31

**Evidence:**

> DHS proposes to amend its regulations by changing the admission period in the F, J, and I classifications from duration of status to an admission for a fixed time period... Nonimmigrants who would like to stay in the United States beyond their fixed date of admission would need to apply directly to DHS for an extension of stay (EOS).

> DHS proposes changes to the admission provisions for these nonimmigrant classifications, including replacing admissions for 'duration of status' with a fixed admission period... up to a maximum period of 4 years or the length of program, whichever is shorter, plus 30 days arrival and 30 days departure periods.

## 3. Extension of Stay (EOS) Requirement and Procedures for F, J, and I Nonimmigrants

**Affected groups:** F nonimmigrants, J exchange visitors, I nonimmigrants

**Current policy:** F and J nonimmigrants are not generally required to file for EOS, with program extensions handled by DSOs or Responsible Officers (ROs) without DHS adjudication; I nonimmigrants admitted for D/S with no fixed requirement to file EOS.

**Proposed change:**

Require F, J, and I nonimmigrants to file EOS applications with USCIS to remain beyond their fixed admission period. Remove current exemptions for F and J from EOS filing. USCIS will collect biometrics and may conduct interviews. Immigration officers will adjudicate EOS applications verifying eligibility and compliance. Timely filing is mandatory to maintain status. No grace period will be given upon EOS denial—immediate departure required.

**Plain English:**

Students, exchange visitors, and foreign media representatives must now formally apply to DHS to extend their stay beyond the fixed period. They will give fingerprints and possibly be interviewed. If their application is denied once their stay expires, they must leave immediately.

**Allowed:** Filing EOS applications using forms designated by USCIS, Biometric collection and possible USCIS interviews during EOS adjudication, Continuation of authorized employment and training under pending EOS in certain categories

**Restricted or prohibited:** Remaining beyond authorized stay without filing timely EOS, Unlawful presence accrual after fixed admission expiration without approved EOS, Staying beyond EOS denial without departing immediately

**Exceptions:** Certain representatives of foreign governments and their families (A-1, A-2) exempt from EOS filing, Transition provisions for current D/S status holders

**Time limits / durations:** Not stated

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1(c)(2), 8 CFR 214.1(c)(3)(v), 8 CFR 214.1(c)(5), 8 CFR 103.16, 8 CFR 103.2(b)(9)

**Source pages:** 3, 13

**Evidence:**

> DHS proposes to strike the phrase exempting F and J nonimmigrants from filing an EOS, requiring them to file an EOS application if they wish to remain in the U.S. beyond their admission period.

> As part of the EOS application, USCIS requires biometric collection and may require an interview to confirm identity and assess eligibility.

## 4. Automatic Extension of F-1 Status and Employment Authorization During Pending EOS

**Affected groups:** F-1 students with timely filed EOS applications

**Current policy:** F-1 students have employment authorization tied to admission period; extension of employment authorization is limited and often requires separate approvals.

**Proposed change:**

Authorize automatic extension of F-1 status and certain employment authorizations (on-campus employment, CPT, off-campus employment for severe economic hardship) for up to 240 days while EOS applications are pending. Applications filed during the 30-day grace period after program completion do not get automatic employment extension.

**Plain English:**

If an F-1 student applies on time for an extension, their status and some employment authorizations automatically extend for up to 240 days while waiting for USCIS decision. No automatic work permission if application is filed during the 30-day grace period after studies.

**Allowed:** Automatic extension of status and employment authorizations for up to 240 days during EOS pendency, Continued work per conditions during EOS pendency

**Restricted or prohibited:** No automatic employment authorization if EOS application filed during 30-day grace period after program end, Employment ceases if EOS is denied or pendency exceeds 240 days

**Exceptions:** Severe economic hardship employment may have specific different extension allowances

**Time limits / durations:** Up to 240 days automatic extension

**CFR references:** proposed 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(viii), 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(6)(i),(iii), 8 CFR 274a.12(c)(3)(iii)

**Source pages:** 3, 21, 41

**Evidence:**

> DHS proposes to allow aliens in F–1 status to continue authorized employment while their EOS applications are pending, not to exceed a period of 240 days.

> An F–1 student whose status has expired but timely filed an extension is considered to be in authorized stay until a decision is made.

## 5. Limits on Educational Program Changes and Transfer Restrictions for F-1 Students

**Affected groups:** F-1 students

**Current policy:** Students admitted under D/S can change programs or transfer schools with fewer restrictions; no aggregate limits on language study duration.

**Proposed change:**

Require F-1 students to complete their first academic year at initial school before transferring unless SEVP authorizes exceptions. Prohibit graduate students from changing programs during study. Disallow maintaining or being admitted for a program at the same or lower educational level after completion; progression must be to higher educational levels only. Limit language training students to a maximum aggregate of 24 months including breaks. Reduce post-completion departure preparation from 60 to 30 days.

**Plain English:**

F-1 students must stay one academic year before transferring unless exceptions apply, can't change graduate programs mid-study, can't enroll at the same or lower education level after finishing a program, language students can only study up to 24 months total, and must leave within 30 days after finishing studies.

**Allowed:** Transfers or changes after first academic year with SEVP authorization, Progression to higher educational levels only

**Restricted or prohibited:** Transfers or educational changes within first academic year without authorization, Graduate-level students prohibited from changing programs mid-study, Maintaining F-1 status via enrollment in same or lower level after program completion, Language training beyond aggregate 24 months, Departure preparation period longer than 30 days after studies

**Exceptions:** SEVP may authorize exceptions for extenuating circumstances such as school closures or emergencies

**Time limits / durations:** First academic year required before transfer, 24 months aggregate limit on language training stay, 30 days departure preparation period after studies

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(i), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(v)

**Source pages:** 3, 17, 18, 19

**Evidence:**

> For F–1 students changing educational objectives or transferring to an SEVP-certified school, requiring completion of the first academic year unless SEVP authorizes an exception; prohibiting graduate students from changing programs mid-study; limiting language training to 24 months aggregate including breaks; decreasing departure preparation period from 60 to 30 days.

## 6. Change of Status Application Abandonment Upon Departure

**Affected groups:** Aliens applying for change of status to F or J status

**Current policy:** Change of status applications remain pending even if alien departs U.S.; policy not codified.

**Proposed change:**

Codify that if an alien timely files an application to change to another nonimmigrant status but departs the United States while application is pending, USCIS will consider the application abandoned.

**Plain English:**

If you apply to change your visa status but leave the U.S. before a decision, USCIS will treat your application as abandoned.

**Allowed:** Underlying petitions filed with change of status application may still be approved, Alien may apply for visa at U.S. consulate and be admitted under new classification

**Restricted or prohibited:** Change of status applications are abandoned if alien departs while application is pending

**Exceptions:** Not stated

**Time limits / durations:** Not stated

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 248.1, 8 CFR 248.1(f)

**Source pages:** 3, 31, 46

**Evidence:**

> Codifying the long-standing policy under which DHS deems abandoned an application to change to another nonimmigrant status if the alien departs the U.S. while the application is pending.

> If an alien timely files an application to change status but departs while pending, USCIS will consider the application abandoned.

## 7. Fixed Admission and Extension Periods for I Nonimmigrants with Special Provisions for PRC Passport Holders

**Affected groups:** I nonimmigrants, I nonimmigrants presenting passports from the People’s Republic of China (PRC) except Hong Kong and Macau SAR passports

**Current policy:** I nonimmigrants admitted with Duration of Status; no codified fixed admission or extension periods.

**Proposed change:**

Replace D/S with fixed admission periods for I nonimmigrants not exceeding 240 days or length necessary to complete assignment. PRC passport holders limited to 90-day maximum admission and extension periods. ESO applications required to remain beyond fixed period, with biometric submission and fees. Activities consistent with I classification may continue during timely pending EOS up to 240 or 90 days respectively. If EOS pending longer, work must cease until approved. Denial requires immediate departure with no post-denial grace period. Dependent I nonimmigrants are subject to similar conditions.

**Plain English:**

I visa holders get up to 240 days to stay (90 days for most PRC passport holders). They must file timely EOS applications to continue working and staying. If extensions are denied or pending longer than allowed, they must stop working and leave immediately. Dependents follow same rules.

**Allowed:** Initial admission and extensions up to 240 days (90 days for PRC passport holders, except HK and Macau), Continuing work and activities consistent with I classification while timely EOS applications are pending, Multiple consecutive EOS applications if timely filed

**Restricted or prohibited:** Admission and extension periods exceeding 240 or 90 days as applicable, Working beyond allowed pending EOS period without approval, Failure to depart immediately after EOS denial

**Exceptions:** PRC passport holders (except HK and Macau SAR passports) limited to 90 days admission and extension

**Time limits / durations:** Admission and EOS validity up to 240 days or activity completion, 90 days limit for certain PRC passport holders, Automatic extension up to 240 or 90 days during timely EOS pendency

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.2(i)(3)(i), 8 CFR 214.2(i)(3)(ii), 8 CFR 214.2(i)(5)(i), 8 CFR 214.2(i)(5)(iii), 8 CFR 214.2(i)(6)

**Source pages:** 3, 16, 29, 31, 43

**Evidence:**

> Replacing D/S for I nonimmigrants with admission for a fixed time period... not to exceed 240 days with the exception of some I aliens from the People’s Republic of China who have 90 days.

> Aliens seeking admission in I status may be admitted up to 240 days, except PRC passports limited to 90 days...continuing activities while timely EOS applications are pending up to these time periods.

> If EOS remains pending beyond this period, alien must cease working until approved.

## 8. Fixed Admission and Extension Periods for F Nonimmigrants Including Program Extension Requirements

**Affected groups:** F nonimmigrant students and their F-2 dependents

**Current policy:** F-1 students admitted for Duration of Status without fixed maximum admission period; program extensions granted by DSOs without USCIS adjudication.

**Proposed change:**

Admit F-1 students for a fixed period to complete their program per Form I-20, not exceeding 4 years. English language training capped at 24 months total, public high school attendance capped at 12 months. Students must apply for EOS with USCIS to extend stay beyond admission period and provide compelling academic reasons, documented medical illness, or exceptional circumstances. Prohibit program or school changes in first academic year without SEVP exception; prohibit graduate level changes during study; disallow enrolling in same or lower educational level after completion. F-2 dependents' stay limited to principal's stay. Extensions adjudicated by USCIS requiring biometrics and supporting documentation. EOS denial requires immediate departure with no grace period.

**Plain English:**

F-1 students get fixed admission periods up to 4 years to complete studies, must apply for USCIS extensions with valid reasons, and face restrictions on program changes. Dependents cannot stay longer than students. Extensions require USCIS review and biometrics. Denied extensions require immediate leave.

**Allowed:** Fixed admission tied to program length up to 4 years, Extensions based on compelling academic, medical, or exceptional reasons, Limited transfers and educational changes with SEVP authorization, F-2 dependents stay concurrent with principal

**Restricted or prohibited:** Admission for Duration of Status (D/S) removed, Language training beyond 24 months aggregate, Public high school attendance beyond 12 months, Graduate students changing programs during study, Program changes within first academic year without SEVP exception, Enrollment in same or lower educational level after program completion, Failure to file timely EOS, No grace period after EOS denial

**Exceptions:** SEVP exceptions for transfers or educational changes under extenuating circumstances, Certain students may delay or suspend program change restrictions, Transition provisions for students maintaining status before rule effective date

**Time limits / durations:** Admission up to 4 years or program length, 24 months aggregate limit for language students, 12 months aggregate limit for public high school students, Extensions not exceeding 4 years generally, 30-day departure period after program or OPT, Timely EOS filing on or before admission expiration including 30-day grace period

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(i), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(7), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(8), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(v)

**Source pages:** 3, 17, 18, 19, 22, 23, 24, 26, 38, 41, 42

**Evidence:**

> An F–1 student is admitted for a fixed period, not to exceed 4 years, plus additional times noted... Language training students restricted to aggregate 24 months including breaks; graduate students may not change programs mid-course; no enrollment at same or lower educational level after completion.

> DSOs may no longer grant extensions based on normal progress; USCIS adjudicates EOS applications requiring evidence including medical documentation and financial support.

> The authorized period of stay for F–2 dependents may not exceed that of the principal F–1 alien.

> F–1 students must file timely EOS applications; dependents must file own or included applications; failure to timely file results in needing reinstatement.

> If USCIS denies EOS and admission period expired, immediate departure required with no grace period.

## 9. Fixed Admission and Extension Periods for J Nonimmigrants and Their Dependents

**Affected groups:** J-1 exchange visitors and J-2 dependents

**Current policy:** J-1 nonimmigrants admitted for Duration of Status; program extensions authorized by Responsible Officers and DOS; J-2 employment authorized separately.

**Proposed change:**

Admit J-1s for fixed periods up to 4 years or program end date per Form DS-2019. Extensions require filing EOS applications with USCIS including biometrics and a valid DS-2019. J-2 dependents may stay only up to principal J-1's admission period; employment requires USCIS authorization with conditions limiting support of J-1 principal. J-1s with timely filed EOS may continue authorized activities and employment up to 240 days beyond admission expiration. Denial of EOS mandates immediate departure. Travel while EOS pending does not abandon application. J-1 status may be extended automatically during H-1B adjudication.

**Plain English:**

J-1 exchange visitors will get a fixed stay period (up to 4 years) and must apply to extend with USCIS. Their family members can stay and work only with authorization and only to support family activities, not the principal. Visitors can keep working while waiting on extension decisions for up to 240 days. If extension is denied, they must leave immediately. They can travel without losing pending extension applications. Extensions needed before H-1B approval can be granted.

**Allowed:** Admission up to 4 years or program end date, Extension applications to USCIS with fees, biometrics, and valid DS-2019, J-2 employment with USCIS authorization limited to family recreational or cultural support, Continuation of employment/training while timely EOS pending up to 240 days, Travel without abandoning EOS application, Automatic extension for pending H-1B petitions

**Restricted or prohibited:** Admission for Duration of Status removed, J-2 employment only allowed with specific USCIS authorization, Extension beyond principal’s admission period prohibited, Immediate departure required after EOS denial

**Exceptions:** Not stated

**Time limits / durations:** Admission and extensions up to 4 years, 30-day grace periods before and after program, Up to 240 days continuation of activities while EOS pending, Extensions renewed in 6-month increments as established by Federal Register

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.2(j)

**Source pages:** 9, 27, 36, 43, 45

**Evidence:**

> J–1 exchange visitors may be admitted for a fixed period not to exceed 4 years, with a 30-day grace period before and after program for departure or status maintenance.

> J–1 nonimmigrants must file EOS applications with USCIS with required fees, biometrics, and valid DS–2019. J–2 dependents may engage in employment only with USCIS authorization and income restrictions apply.

> J–1 individuals with timely filed EOS applications may continue employment or training up to 240 days beyond status expiration.

> Travel during pending EOS is not considered abandonment.

> Automatic extension of J–1 status may be granted during pending H–1B petition adjudication.

## 10. Discretionary Visa Revalidation for Short Travel in Contiguous Territory and Adjacent Islands

**Affected groups:** F, J, and M nonimmigrants traveling in contiguous territory or adjacent islands up to 30 days

**Current policy:** Regulatory text uses 'shall' indicating CBP must admit aliens and extend visa automatically after travel of up to 30 days in contiguous territories or adjacent islands.

**Proposed change:**

Change language from 'shall' to 'may' to clarify CBP officers have discretion to admit or deny such aliens and determine admission period and visa validity extensions.

**Plain English:**

CBP officers will have discretion whether to admit nonimmigrants returning from short trips (up to 30 days) in nearby countries or islands and whether to extend their visa validity automatically, instead of mandatory admission.

**Allowed:** Automatic visa validity extension for short absences up to 30 days, CBP discretion on alien admissibility and admission period

**Restricted or prohibited:** Remove requirement for mandatory admission by CBP in these circumstances

**Exceptions:** Not stated

**Time limits / durations:** Absences not exceeding 30 days

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1(b)(1), 22 CFR 41.112(d)

**Source pages:** 13

**Evidence:**

> DHS proposes to change language from 'shall' to 'may' clarifying CBP’s discretion in admission and visa validity extension for excursions up to 30 days in contiguous territory or adjacent islands.

## 11. Updated Reporting Requirements for J-1 Exchange Visitors and Responsible Officers

**Affected groups:** J-1 exchange visitors, Responsible Officers (ROs)

**Current policy:** J-1 exchange visitors must report address changes within 10 calendar days; ROs update SEVIS within 21 days; USCIS referred to as 'Service'.

**Proposed change:**

Replace 'Service' with 'USCIS'; require exchange visitors to report legal name and address changes within 10 calendar days to program sponsor; require ROs to update SEVIS within 10 business days; remove references to non-SEVIS programs.

**Plain English:**

J-1 visitors must report address or legal name changes promptly; sponsors must update SEVIS quickly. Terminology updated to USCIS. Non-SEVIS program references removed.

**Allowed:** Reporting within specified times

**Restricted or prohibited:** Not stated

**Exceptions:** Not stated

**Time limits / durations:** 10 calendar days for visitors to report changes, 10 business days for ROs to update SEVIS

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.2(j)(1)(ix)

**Source pages:** 27, 45

**Evidence:**

> Exchange visitors must inform DHS and responsible officers of name or address changes within 10 calendar days, who in turn must update SEVIS within 10 business days.

## 12. Employment Authorization for Foreign Information Media Representatives (I Nonimmigrants)

**Affected groups:** I nonimmigrants (foreign information media representatives), Dependents of such representatives

**Proposed change:**

Clarify that I nonimmigrants may be employed in freelance or self-employment situations if they meet requirements of working for a qualifying foreign media organization; employment authorization does not extend to dependents.

**Plain English:**

Foreign media visa holders can work freelance or for themselves if they prove they work for a qualifying foreign media organization; their dependents cannot work.

**Allowed:** Employment for sponsoring foreign news agency, Freelance or self-employment for qualifying foreign media organizations

**Restricted or prohibited:** Employment authorization does not extend to dependents

**Exceptions:** Not stated

**Time limits / durations:** Not stated

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(10), 8 CFR 214.2(i)

**Source pages:** 31, 46

**Evidence:**

## 13. Transition Period for F and J Nonimmigrants Currently Admitted for D/S

**Affected groups:** F and J nonimmigrants admitted for Duration of Status (D/S) on final rule effective date

**Current policy:** Such nonimmigrants remain admitted under D/S without filing EOS.

**Proposed change:**

Allow F and J nonimmigrants admitted on final rule effective date to remain in status without filing EOS through program end date on their Form I-20 or DS-2019, not to exceed 4 years from effective date, plus 60 days (F) or 30 days (J) for departure. Applications for certain post-completion OPT filed within 6 months post-effective date exempt from EOS filing during transition.

**Plain English:**

People already here under the old D/S system can stay until their current program ends (up to 4 years), plus extra departure days. After they leave and return, they must follow the new fixed admission and EOS rules.

**Allowed:** Remaining in status without EOS until program end date or 4 years from effective date plus departure period, Filing post-completion OPT or STEM OPT employment authorization within 6 months without EOS requirement

**Restricted or prohibited:** Failure to file EOS after transition period ends, Departing and reentering triggers fixed admission and EOS rules

**Exceptions:** Certain F-1 nonimmigrants present 60 days after effective date filing OPT/STEM OPT within 6 months are exempt from EOS filing

**Time limits / durations:** Up to 4 years from final rule effective date, 60-day departure period for F nonimmigrants, 30-day departure period for J nonimmigrants

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1(m)(1), 8 CFR 214.1(m)(2), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(B)(2), 8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(C)

**Source pages:** 15, 36

**Evidence:**

> DHS proposes to allow F and J nonimmigrants present on the final rule’s effective date admitted for D/S to remain without filing EOS up to the program end date on their Form I–20 or DS–2019, not to exceed 4 years from effective date, plus departure time.

> Timely filed applications for post-completion OPT within 6 months after effective date exempt filing EOS during transition.
