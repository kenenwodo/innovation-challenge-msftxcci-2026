## 1. Fixed Time Period Admissions for F, J, and I Nonimmigrants

**Affected groups:** F academic students, J exchange visitors, I representatives of foreign information media

**Current policy:** Admitted for 'duration of status' (D/S), an unspecified admission period as long as complying with the classification.

**Proposed change:** Replace duration of status (D/S) with admissions for a fixed time period requiring nonimmigrants to apply to DHS for extensions of stay (EOS) if they want to stay beyond the fixed period.

**Plain English:** Instead of staying in the U.S. as long as they follow the rules (D/S), F, J, and most I visa holders will get a fixed period to stay and must apply to extend their stay if needed.

**Allowed:** Extensions of stay upon application, Employment authorization extensions under certain conditions

**Restricted or prohibited:** Indefinite stays based on D/S framework, Staying beyond authorized period without EOS approval

**Exceptions:** I nonimmigrants from People's Republic of China have a limited 90-day time period admission

**Time limits / durations:** Admissions for F and J limited up to 4 years, I nonimmigrants limited up to 240 days, except for some from PRC with up to 90 days, 30-day preparation to depart after course completion (F-1), Up to 240 days employment authorization extension while EOS pending

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1, 8 CFR 214.2, 8 CFR 248.1, 8 CFR 274a.12

**Source page:** 1

**Evidence:**

> DHS proposes to amend its regulations by changing the admission period in the F, J, and I classifications from duration of status to an admission for a fixed time period...Nonimmigrants who would like to stay in the United States beyond their fixed date of admission would need to apply directly to DHS for an extension of stay (EOS).

## 2. Revised Extension of Stay (EOS) Application Procedures

**Affected groups:** F, J, and I nonimmigrants

**Current policy:** Extensions granted during D/S status without a formal fixed admission period.

**Proposed change:** Outline a specific process for EOS applications for F, J, and I nonimmigrants including biometric collection requirements and automatic employment authorization extensions up to 240 days for F nonimmigrants with timely filed EOS applications.

**Plain English:** Nonimmigrants will apply for stay extensions with clear steps including providing biometrics; students can keep working up to 240 days if their extension is pending.

**Allowed:** Auto-extension of employment authorization up to 240 days for F nonimmigrants under economic hardship, Employment continuation for J-1 and I nonimmigrants while EOS pending

**Restricted or prohibited:** Employment authorization expiration ends work authorization for J-2 dependents once EAD expires

**Exceptions:** I nonimmigrants from PRC have special provisions related to admission length and EOS

**Time limits / durations:** Auto-extension of EAD up to 240 days or regulatory notice period, EOS application period aligned with fixed admission periods

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1, 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(v), 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(20)

**Source page:** 3

**Evidence:**

> Outlining the process for EOS applications for F, J, and I nonimmigrants; ...Providing for collection of biometric information in conjunction with an EOS application for F, J, and I nonimmigrants as may be required by 8 CFR 103.16; ...Allowing F nonimmigrants whose timely filed EOS applications remain pending ... to receive an auto-extension of their current authorization for on-campus and off-campus employment based on severe economic hardship resulting from emergent circumstances under existing 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(v).

## 3. Limits on Educational Program Changes and Stay Durations in F-1 Status

**Affected groups:** F-1 students

**Current policy:** Students admitted under D/S with flexibility to change programs; language training students have less clear aggregate limits.

**Proposed change:** Require F-1 students to complete their first academic year at the initial school before transferring unless SEVP authorizes an exception; prohibit graduate students from changing programs during study; restrict changes to same or lower educational level; limit language training students to 24 months aggregate stay; reduce allowed departure preparation period from 60 to 30 days after completion of study or training.

**Plain English:** F-1 students must stay one academic year before transferring schools, can't change graduate programs mid-study, can't move to equal or lower levels once a program is completed, language students limited to 2 years total, and must leave within 30 days after finishing studies.

**Allowed:** One academic year minimum before transfer (with exceptions), Change to higher educational level programs only after program completion

**Restricted or prohibited:** Graduate level students prohibited from changing programs mid-course, Reducing departure preparation time to 30 days, Language training limited to 24 months total

**Exceptions:** Transfer exceptions authorized by SEVP

**Time limits / durations:** 24-month aggregate limit for language training students including breaks, 30 days to prepare to depart after course or training completion

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.2(f)

**Source page:** 3

**Evidence:**

> For F–1 students changing educational objectives or transferring to an SEVP-certified school, requiring that the student complete his or her first academic year ... unless an exception is authorized by SEVP; Prohibiting F–1 students at the graduate education level from changing programs at any point during a program of study; ...Limiting language training students to an aggregate 24-month period of stay, including breaks and an annual vacation; Decreasing from 60 to 30 days the allowed period for F–1 nonimmigrants to prepare to depart from the United States after completion of a course of study or authorized period of post-completion practical training.

## 4. Clarification and Codification of I Nonimmigrant Status Requirements

**Affected groups:** I nonimmigrants, Dependents of I nonimmigrants

**Current policy:** I status granted for duration of foreign employment duties without fixed end date apart from some PRC exceptions; ambiguous definitions and evidence requirements.

**Proposed change:** Replace D/S with fixed time period admissions for I nonimmigrants not exceeding 240 days (except some PRC nationals with 90 days); codify definition of foreign media organizations consistent with existing USCIS and DOS practice; update evidence required for I nonimmigrant eligibility; clarify continued work authorization during EOS pendency for I nonimmigrants under 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(20).

**Plain English:** I visa holders get set stay times up to 240 days and must reapply if extended. DHS defines foreign media groups clearly and specifies what documents are needed. I visa holders can keep working if their extension is being processed.

**Allowed:** Working for foreign employer during EOS pendency up to 240 days, Admission periods up to 240 days for I nonimmigrants

**Restricted or prohibited:** Admission lasting longer than 240 days without extension, D/S admissions for I nonimmigrants replaced

**Exceptions:** I nonimmigrants from PRC limited to 90-day fixed admission period

**Time limits / durations:** 240 days maximum admission period generally; 90 days for some PRC nationals

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 214.1, 8 CFR 214.2, 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(20)

**Source page:** 3

**Evidence:**

> Replacing D/S for I nonimmigrants with admission for a fixed time period until they complete the activities or assignments consistent with the I classification, not to exceed 240 days (with the exception of some I aliens from the People’s Republic of China), with an EOS available for I nonimmigrants who can meet specified EOS requirements; Codifying the definition of a foreign media organization for I nonimmigrant status, consistent with long-standing USCIS and U.S. Department of State (DOS) practice; Clarifying that I nonimmigrants are authorized to continue working in the United States for their foreign employer, under 8 CFR 274a.12(b)(20), while their timely filed EOS application is pending for up to 240 days.

## 5. Change of Status Application Abandonment upon Departure

**Affected groups:** F and J nonimmigrants applying for change of status

**Current policy:** Policy on abandonment of change of status applications when alien departs is not codified.

**Proposed change:** Codify policy that DHS deems an application to change nonimmigrant status, including change to F or J status, abandoned if the alien departs the United States while the application is pending.

**Plain English:** If a person applies to change their visa status and leaves the U.S. before a decision, their application is considered abandoned.

**Allowed:** Not stated

**Restricted or prohibited:** Change of status application remains pending only if alien stays in U.S.; departing abandons application.

**Exceptions:** Not stated

**Time limits / durations:** Not stated

**CFR references:** 8 CFR 248.1

**Source page:** 3

**Evidence:**

> Codifying the long-standing policy under which DHS deems abandoned an application to change to another nonimmigrant status, including F or J status, if the alien who timely filed the application departs the United States while the application is pending.
